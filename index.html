011879 – 1894

Early Years in Ulm and Munich

Albert Einstein was born on March 14, 1879, in Ulm, in the Kingdom of Württemberg. His father Hermann was a salesman and engineer; his mother Pauline introduced him to music. At age five, a pocket compass transfixed him: the needle moved, yet nothing touched it. This invisible force sparked a lifelong obsession with the hidden laws of nature. He taught himself geometry and calculus from textbooks and grew increasingly uncomfortable with the rigid, military style of his school.

021896 – 1900

The Polytechnic Years in Zurich

After completing secondary school in Aarau, Einstein enrolled at the Federal Polytechnic School in Zurich. He was brilliant but unconventional, often skipping lectures to study theoretical physics on his own. At seventeen, he imagined riding alongside a beam of light — the seed of special relativity. He graduated in 1900, but his independence and impatience with professors cost him an academic position.

031902 – 1909

The Bern Patent Office

Einstein found work as a technical expert at the Federal Patent Office in Bern. The humble job gave him a quiet desk, steady income, and time to think. In 1905, his annus mirabilis, he published four papers on the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion, special relativity, and mass-energy equivalence. He was twenty-six, a patent clerk with no academic affiliation, transforming physics forever.

041914 – 1919

General Relativity and World Fame

In November 1915, Einstein presented the field equations of general relativity: gravity was not a Newtonian force, but the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy. In 1919, Arthur Eddington's eclipse expedition confirmed that starlight bent around the Sun as Einstein predicted. The theorist became an overnight global celebrity.

051921 – 1932

Nobel Prize and the Quantum Debate

Einstein received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for the photoelectric effect. He traveled the world, became a household name, and debated Niels Bohr over the meaning of quantum mechanics. Though one of quantum theory's founders, he never accepted its probabilistic nature: "God does not play dice." He spent the decade searching for a unified field theory.

061933 – 1955

Exile in America and Final Years

When Hitler came to power, Einstein never returned to Germany. He settled at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton and became an American citizen in 1940. After Hiroshima, he advocated nuclear disarmament, civil rights, and world government. He continued his unified field quest until his death on April 18, 1955, at age seventy-six.
